The playbook
  1. Start before the first reading. The median ordinance passed — days after it first appeared in the minutes, so there is little time once an item is on the agenda. Talk to the member who leads on the issue (see the lead map) before it is introduced.
  2. Bring amendment language, not only opposition. — of ordinances were amended before passage, usually by a substitute version offered by a member.
  3. Count to —. That many yeas carried every split vote this year. Members marked "usually in the majority" decide close votes; two members who reliably vote together can move a third.
  4. Read a delay as negotiation, not defeat. Of delayed items that came back for a recorded vote, — were adopted. A layover is a window to resolve concerns, often raised by the member who moved it.
  5. Get written comments into the record early. Per the minutes, comments on public-hearing items received by noon the day before a meeting are attached to the public record (Contact-Council@ci.stpaul.mn.us or CouncilHearing@ci.stpaul.mn.us).
  6. Read every item through all six PERSIA lenses. An economic item with a social or political dimension (housing rules, appointments, ballot questions) is where the council has split; a purely economic or artistic item (grants, fees, sound permits) almost never does. Frame asks in the lens the lead member works in most (see the hexagons).
  7. Watch the issues that split. Housing and tenant rules, land-use appeals and economic-development appointments drew most of the business-related split votes; routine grants, fees and licenses almost always pass without dissent.

How this is measured
  • Source: St. Paul City Council minutes PDFs (stpaul.legistar.com), January 7 – October 28, 2026. Votes come from roll calls; motions and remarks come from the minutes' narrative.
  • "Business-related" items are tagged from item titles by keyword into eight issue areas: labor and wages; housing and tenant rules; economic development and incentives; licensing and enforcement; land use and permitting; utilities, franchises and fees; taxes, levies and assessments; and events and sound permits. Property-appeal hearings and consent-agenda blocks are left out.
  • PERSIA lenses (Political, Economic, Religious, Social, Intellectual, Artistic) are assigned from item titles by keyword. An item can carry several lenses; property appeals count as Social, and infrastructure, land use and code changes as Economic or Political. Religious items are few (church property petitions, faith-based event permits, religious observances).
  • "Usually in the majority" means a member sided with the majority on at least 80% of all split votes they cast this year.
  • Ordinance timing runs from the first meeting where the ordinance appears in the minutes to the meeting where it was adopted. Items adopted inside a consent agenda after a delay have no separate vote and are counted as "no separate vote recorded later".
  • Members usually move items in their own ward, so the lead map partly reflects geography. Past actions don't predict votes on new items; use this to decide whom to brief and when.
